THE TWO INDICATORS
Center of Gravity (CoG) — Belkhayate's polynomial-regression channel over the last ~180 bars (original D1/H4 setting): thick blue centre, then bands at 1σ (dotted), 2σ (dashed red / green = the signal lines) and 3σ (solid). Price at the dashed line = market "excessively deflected" from its mean → high odds of a return to the centre.
Candle Average (CA) — histogram of the current candle's size relative to the average of the last few candles. Reading beyond ±0.81 = the candle is "longer than usual". Up-bar = bullish spike, down-bar = bearish spike.
Together: price far from the mean and candles longer than normal = the probability of the return move rises. Both readings must be "now" — CoG is a regression that repaints: the historical band you see later is not the band you saw at the time (the author shows signals that "disappeared"). Real-time only.
THE SIGNAL — THREE CONDITIONS AT ONCE (short example; long = mirror)
TREND = SLOPE OF THE OUTER LINES
Only the outer lines' slope decides direction. Recently turned lines = risky signal ("the lines turned up only a moment ago"). Slope must have been in place through a proper trend, not a few bars. The author adds a structure check: in a down-trend the market must keep making lower highs / lower lows — if the last swing high was broken, there is no short even with a perfect CoG + CA signal.

WHEN TO SKIP A VALID-LOOKING SIGNAL (the runaway-trade filters)
The book's stated goal after learning the system: eliminate "runaway" trades ("zlecenia uciekające" — 120 pips under water for 10 days, still closed at TP) and minimise time in the market.
1After a very long move — hoping for continuation is a trend-reversal risk
2Into a strong S/R or trend line — price can bounce and turn there; TP set beyond a level is the classic mistake
3After a long consolidation — the first move out is often false; enter only on the confirmed continuation
4Lines flat, or turned only recently — no signal / weak signal
5Structure broken — new higher high in a 'down-trend' = the trend rule is violated; skip
6CoG squeeze after a violent bar — trends often end with a spike that pinches the channel; skip despite the signal
7Strong impulse still running — 'if the market falls hard and there is a signal, do not enter until it stops'; a better entry, not against momentum
8No CA reading — band touch alone is not a signal
9Pair unsuitable — strong trenders with shallow corrections (author's example: USD/JPY) never give the return move; prefer 'ragged', choppy pairs

STOP LOSS · RISK · WHAT THE AUTHOR HONESTLY SAYS
SL: a fixed 300 pips (H4/D1 original). Version 2 has no SL. The author knows traders who run it that way (M1 statement: +150 % in 130 trades, account ×6 in 3 months — with ≈ $2k of open losers on a $3k account).
His reservation: "you cannot manage risk properly in it"; a runaway entry can sit at a loss for weeks or take the account to margin call. He trades it anyway, with the filters above, because the return-to-mean edge is real on choppy pairs.
Pairs: start with 6 (the original set had 20); choppy, mean-reverting ones; gold was the original instrument.
SEA verdict on SL: "no SL" is not codeable — Policy-A risk sizing needs a stop. Proposed: SL beyond the 3σ outer line (+ cushion) or ATR-based, sized to 1–2 % — this is the one place the preset must deviate from the book, and it should say so.
